The haar, 05:40 57°38′N 3°19′W

We don't make whisky. The weather does.

Most mornings between April and September a cold fog rolls in off the North Sea and settles over our warehouses. It keeps the casks cool, damp and slow. Twelve years of it is what you taste.

One spring rises through old red sandstone, then peat, then gravel. We have never treated it, and we have never needed to.

Maturation inside the fog is slower, damper and more forgiving than four miles inland. The angels take 1.8% a year and no more.

Cold stone and orchard fruit. Dried citrus peel with a thread of woodsmoke carried in off the water.

Toasted oak and dark honey. Warm spice arrives late, held inside a broad and unhurried body.

Long and dry. Vanilla receding into salt air, the smoke the last thing to leave the glass.
